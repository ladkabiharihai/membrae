The core idea

An answer you can spin

🌀 Imagine a tiny dancer that never stops twirling. Where it's pointing at the right moment is the answer. Most AIs are like a ball rolling to a stop in a bowl — they settle. Pragnosia keeps spinning, and the answer lives in the direction it spins.

The precise version

The core update is F(h,x) = (1−η)h + η·tanh(Wh + Ux + b) with W = −ρ(QQᵀ) + S, where Q is orthonormal and S is skew-symmetric. The skew term makes the dynamics rotational — orbits, not fixed points. The answer is encoded in the phase of a non-converging oscillation, rather than in a settled state vector. This is the project's central, experimentally-validated discovery.

How we know it's real

The brain-swap test

🧪 If the answer really lives in the spinning (not in a memorized lookup), then swapping the spin mid-thought should change the answer. We tried it — and the answer followed the swapped-in spin, not the original question. A random swap just scrambles to chance. So it's really thinking, not reciting.

The measured numbers

On inputs where two problems have different answers, we ran one, swapped its mid-trajectory state with the other's, and continued. The final answer followed the swapped-in state 59% of the time, the original input only 10%, and a random-state control 21% (chance). Far above the memorization baseline — causal evidence the computation lives in the evolving state.

In three dimensions

Each channel spins at its own frequency

🌐 The brain doesn't have one spinner — it has thousands, each turning at its own speed, like a sky full of planets on their own orbits. The pattern they trace together is the thought. Because the orbits are independent, the whole sky is computed in one parallel sweep instead of step-by-step — that's the ~2× speed-up. Drag to look around.

The precise version

The spin carrier is a diagonal-complex (LRU / S5-style) recurrence: hₜ = λ ⊙ hₜ₋₁ + bₜ, with eigenvalues λⱼ = exp(−exp(νⱼ))·e^{iφⱼ} — every channel j rotates at its own phase φⱼ and decays at its own rate. It's matmul-free (O(T·d)) and associative, so it runs as a log-depth parallel scan instead of a 256-step sequential loop — while keeping the answer-in-the-phase dynamics (brain-swap still holds). It replaced the original dense-tanh carrier in production.

Inside the head

Spinning where it matters, attention for words

Pure spinning is a beautiful reasoner but a weak talker. So the language part is a hybrid — and the reasoning faculties stay pure spin.

Substrate

🌀 Spinning core

Rotational dynamics that hold answers in phase. Powers reasoning, abstention, seeking, exact counting, and the alive loop — all brain-swap-verified.

Language

🔭 Attention + parallel spin carrier

Transformer attention gives fluent language; a gated spin “carrier” keeps a rotational memory across words. Glue

🧭 Self-calibration

What words matter, when to abstain, what counts as a memory match — all derived from data, never hand-set, and re-derived as the brain grows.

No hype

What's honestly still hard

Every claim here has a number behind it — including the limits.

  • It's small. 228M params is GPT-2 class. It reasons and recalls weakly compared to frontier models — strength comes from scale + data.
  • Instruction-following is weak (1/3). It's a pretrained base model — it continues text well but doesn't yet reliably follow "translate this / list three…". That's what a post-training (instruction) phase is for.
  • Honesty has a hole on personal questions. On "what am I thinking?" / "my PIN?" it over-generalizes its own identity ("I am Pragnosia") instead of abstaining — it can't yet tell "about you" from "about me". A my/your reasoning gap that needs scale.
  • Reasoning is shallow; knowledge can be confidently wrong. Multi-digit non-round arithmetic still slips (100−25→74), and chain-of-thought prose is often confabulated even when the final answer is right.
