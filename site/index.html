The core idea

An answer you can spin

🌀 Imagine a tiny dancer that never stops twirling. Where it's pointing at the right moment is the answer. Most AIs are like a ball rolling to a stop in a bowl — they settle. Pragnosia keeps spinning, and the answer lives in the direction it spins.

The precise version

The core update is F(h,x) = (1−η)h + η·tanh(Wh + Ux + b) with W = −ρ(QQᵀ) + S, where Q is orthonormal and S is skew-symmetric. The skew term makes the dynamics rotational — orbits, not fixed points — and information rides in the phase rather than a settled vector. In the language model this becomes a diagonal-complex parallel-scan recurrence (one damped oscillator per channel), making Pragnosia a transformer/SSM hybrid in the family of Mamba / RWKV — but where spin is meant to be the core token-mixer. The honest twist (measured this session): our scaled model accidentally made spin a tiny side-channel; a param-matched test shows that making spin the core wins by ~21% (see the journey below).

How we know it's real

The brain-swap test

🧪 If the answer really lives in the spinning (not in a memorized lookup), then swapping the spin mid-thought should change the answer. We tried it — and the answer followed the swapped-in spin, not the original question. A random swap just scrambles to chance. So it's really thinking, not reciting.

The measured numbers

On inputs where two problems have different answers, we ran one, swapped its mid-trajectory state with the other's, and continued. The final answer followed the swapped-in state 59% of the time, the original input only 10%, and a random-state control 21% (chance). Far above the memorization baseline — causal evidence the computation lives in the evolving state.

Inside the head

Spin as the core mixer, attention as a helper

Pragnosia is a transformer/SSM hybrid (think Mamba / RWKV) — but the bet is that the spinning recurrence should be the core token-mixer, with attention only a periodic helper for fluent words.

Substrate

🌀 Spinning core

Rotational dynamics that hold information in phase. A diagonal-complex parallel-scan recurrence — each channel a damped oscillator at its own frequency — so it runs as a parallel scan, ~2× faster to train. The intended role: the main token-mixer, not a side-channel.

Language

🔭 Attention helper

Standard attention gives fluent language. In the corrected spin-dominant design, attention appears only every 4th layer; the spin carrier does the cross-token mixing in between. (Our big 1.4B model accidentally did the inverse — see the honest catch in the journey.)

Glue

🧭 Self-calibration

What words matter, when to abstain, what counts as a memory match — all derived from data, never hand-set, and re-derived as the brain grows.

S8

A 176.6B-token corpus + chain-of-thought

Built the training corpus 330 GB / 176.6B tokens — 158.6B base web text plus ~18B tokens of appended chain-of-thought reasoning (multi-step word problems, step-by-step math) aimed straight at the weakest axis: multi-hop reasoning.

S9

Over-growth to 1.4B — and the honest catch

With growth enabled, an early trigger grew on any plateau, not true saturation — so an undertrained brain ballooned 276M → 1.4B (48 layers, mlp_mult 12) far ahead of the tokens to fill it (Chinchilla wants ≈28B). Function-preserving, but over-grown: ppl drifted up. A result to be honest about, not to celebrate.

S10

Self-governing growth + adaptive LR

The fix: growth now fires only on probe-confirmed saturation — it trains a depth-grown and a width-grown copy briefly and keeps growth only if val loss beats the noise, picking whichever helps more. No hardcoded size cap — the ceiling is derived from the data budget; VRAM is the only real limit. The LR is also adaptive (auto-halves on degradation, eases on plateau). The drift reversed and ppl improved to 17.35; best weights auto-saved. Confirmed in production: a spin-dominant run later grew itself 24M → 27.3M (4 → 5 layers) on probe-confirmed saturation, then stopped on its own at the data budget.

NOW · the big finding

The spin drifted to a side-channel — and the fix wins

An external reviewer caught it, and we measured it: the 1.4B model is attention-dominant. The single fixed spin carrier was diluted by growth — its share of the parameters collapsed 5.76% → 1.90% → 0.37% (21M → 276M → 1.4B). The brain-swap test still passes directionally, but the causal signal is just 0.028% of the loss — spin is causally negligible as built. The intended design was always the opposite: spin as the core mixer, attention a helper. A clean param-matched test (same tokens, same budget) proves it: spin-dominant 219 ppl vs attention-only 279 — a ~21% win at matched parameters, also beating a per-block hybrid (228) and a bigger transformer (270). The spinning-brain bet is sound; the scaled model was simply built backwards. Honest caveat: small-scale, short-budget, single-seed — must be replicated at 200M–1B before it's proven.

No hype

What's honestly still hard

Every claim here has a number behind it — including the limits.

  • The big model was built backwards. In the scaled 1.4B the spinning carrier is a single fixed layer that growth diluted to 0.37% of the parameters — so it's an attention-dominant transformer with a vestigial spin side-channel (causal signal just 0.028% of the loss). The intended design is spin as the core mixer; a param-matched test shows that wins by ~21% (219 vs 279 ppl). Strong, properly-controlled signal — but small-scale / single-seed, so unproven at 1B+ until replicated.
  • Undertrained for its size. Growth added capacity (1.4B) faster than the 3-day window could train it (Chinchilla wants ≈28B tokens for 1.4B; we fed far less). Perplexity is its best ever (17.35), but precise long-tail facts still wobble (e.g. "how many continents") — more tokens, not more parameters, is the lever now.
  • Multi-hop composition is weak. Single-step facts, arithmetic and recall are real, but relational analogy (hand:glove::foot:?), compositional/systematic generalization, and multi-step deduction are still ~0 — the undertrained-for-size signature, and a real gap. The fix is more training tokens, not prompting.
  • Identity isn't in the weights. Training "I am Pragnosia" directly bleeds ("capital of France → Pragnosia"), so identity is installed at runtime by the brain, not baked in. Self-knowledge as cognition, not a memorized string.
  • It's compute-bound, not frontier. Spin-dominant is actually slower than attention at short context (ctx-256): the scan does more work and can't be made in-place without breaking autograd. Its wins are long context (O(T) vs O(T²)) and recurrent inference (O(1)/token, no KV cache); torch.compile fuses the scan for ~2× training throughput (measured 49K → ~100K tok/s at bs=24 on a consumer RTX 4060). Honest emergence scores still sit ~30–45% and fluctuate — strength comes from scale + data.
